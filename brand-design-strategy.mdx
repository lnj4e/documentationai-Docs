# Claim Clarity Brand & Design Strategy

This guide aligns product, design, engineering, and marketing around a shared expression of Claim Clarity. Use it when making product, content, or campaign decisions; update it as the product and evidence evolve.

## Strategic Positioning

### Category

**Clinical intelligence infrastructure for healthcare claims.**

Claim Clarity helps teams understand the clinical meaning and documentation behind a claim decision. It connects evidence, policy, and workflow context so people can assess what happened, why it matters, and what to do next.

Position the product as infrastructure that supports informed decisions—not as a generic claims tool, a black-box AI, or a replacement for clinical or operational judgment.

### Positioning statement

For healthcare organizations that need to make sense of complex claims, Claim Clarity is clinical intelligence infrastructure that brings relevant evidence and reasoning into one clear, reviewable experience. Unlike disconnected point solutions or opaque automation, it makes the path from source information to an actionable insight visible to the people responsible for the decision.

### Audience and value

| Audience | What they need | Claim Clarity’s value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clinical and claims reviewers | Understand the evidence and rationale behind a claim | A clear, traceable view of relevant information |
| Operations and revenue-cycle leaders | Identify friction and support consistent workflows | A shared view of issues, decisions, and next steps |
| Technology and data teams | Integrate intelligence into existing systems | A foundation for connected, explainable workflows |
| Executives and partners | Understand impact, accountability, and fit | A credible story grounded in transparency and human oversight |

### Language principles

- Lead with **clinical intelligence infrastructure**; explain the value in plain language immediately afterward.
- Say **supports**, **surfaces**, and **helps teams assess**. Do not imply the system independently makes clinical or coverage decisions.
- Distinguish source facts, policy interpretation, model-generated insights, and human decisions.
- Use specific, verifiable outcomes only when there is evidence and approval to support them.
- Avoid claims such as “eliminates denials,” “guarantees accuracy,” or “fully automates clinical decisions.”

## Brand Principles

1. **Clarity before complexity.** Make dense information understandable without hiding nuance.
2. **Evidence before assertion.** Show where an insight came from and what supports it.
3. **Human judgment stays visible.** Design for review, challenge, and correction—not blind acceptance.
4. **Clinical seriousness, without coldness.** Be calm, precise, and respectful of the people affected.
5. **Confidence through transparency.** Earn trust with traceability, limitations, and meaningful context.

## Visual Identity

### Look and feel

The brand should feel composed, intelligent, and dependable. Prefer a restrained interface with generous spacing, clear hierarchy, and purposeful emphasis. Avoid visual tropes that make the product feel like a hospital monitor, a consumer wellness app, or an autonomous AI.

### Color

The documentation theme establishes these brand colors:

| Token | Value | Recommended role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand teal (light theme) | `#0C8383` | Primary actions, selected states, and key navigation accents |
| Brand teal (dark theme) | `#60ECEC` | Accessible emphasis and interactive accents on dark surfaces |

Use teal as an accent, not as the background for every surface. Pair it with neutral backgrounds and readable foreground text. Reserve semantic colors for meaning: success, caution, error, and informational states must remain distinguishable without relying on color alone. Check contrast in both themes and do not infer a decision outcome from the brand color.

### Typography and layout

- Use the product’s established interface typeface consistently; prioritize legibility over decorative character.
- Create hierarchy through size, weight, spacing, and labels rather than excessive all-caps or many font styles.
- Keep long clinical text comfortably readable, with line lengths and spacing that support scanning.
- Align evidence, rationale, and actions predictably so users can compare information without hunting.
- Use icons as supporting cues and pair them with text for important actions and statuses.

## Product Experience Patterns

### Decision cards

Decision cards summarize a reviewable item without obscuring its detail. Each card should make the following easy to find when applicable:

- **Status:** a neutral, explicit label such as “Needs review” or “Evidence found.”
- **Finding:** a concise statement of what the system surfaced.
- **Why it matters:** the relevant clinical, documentation, or policy context.
- **Evidence:** source references that open the underlying information.
- **Next step:** a clear action, including who is expected to take it.

Keep the summary distinct from the supporting evidence. Avoid presenting a suggested outcome as a final determination. When information is incomplete or conflicting, state that plainly.

### Semantic trace

Show how a conclusion relates to source material. A semantic trace should preserve the link between an insight and its supporting evidence, including source name and location where available. Make it possible to inspect the original context, distinguish direct source text from interpretation, and identify missing or conflicting information.

Do not make an inferred relationship look like a verbatim quotation. If source material cannot be linked or verified, label that limitation rather than implying traceability.

### Confidence

Treat confidence as a qualified signal, not a guarantee of correctness. If a confidence score is shown:

- Explain what the score represents and what it does not represent.
- Pair it with contextual factors or evidence that help a person evaluate it.
- Avoid unsupported precision and universal thresholds.
- Do not use confidence alone to determine a claim outcome or suppress human review.
- Provide an understandable state when confidence is unavailable or inputs are insufficient.

### Explainability and review

Present the key factors behind an insight in an order that matches how a reviewer evaluates it: relevant evidence, applicable context, interpretation, uncertainty, and suggested next step. Provide affordances to inspect sources, flag a problem, and record a human decision where the workflow supports it. Keep system-generated content visibly distinct from user-entered content.

## Marketing Message Pillars

| Pillar | Core message | Supporting proof to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connected intelligence | Bring clinical and claims context together | Demonstrable integrations, connected data, and workflow examples |
| Clear reasoning | Make it easier to understand why an insight appeared | Product examples showing evidence links and reasoning |
| Human-centered decisions | Give reviewers context to make informed decisions | Review controls, escalation paths, and user research |
| Operational readiness | Fit intelligence into real team workflows | Verified implementation details and customer outcomes |

Use proof that is current, representative, and approved. Separate product capabilities from results that depend on customer data, workflow configuration, or human action.

## Competitive Positioning Framework

Compare Claim Clarity by approach and user value rather than by unsupported competitor claims. For each alternative, assess:

| Dimension | Questions to evaluate |
| --- | --- |
| Workflow scope | Does it address a narrow task or connect multiple review steps? |
| Evidence visibility | Can users inspect the source and context behind an insight? |
| Explainability | Are rationale and uncertainty understandable? |
| Human oversight | Can reviewers challenge, correct, and own decisions? |
| Integration | How does it fit existing systems and team processes? |
| Operational fit | What implementation, governance, and support are required? |

Use current public information or documented research for comparisons. State the date and scope of any competitive analysis, and do not claim superiority without substantiation.

## Website Messaging Structure

1. **Hero:** Name the category and outcome in plain language. Example: “Clinical intelligence infrastructure for clearer claims decisions.”
2. **Problem:** Describe the difficulty of connecting clinical context, documentation, and claim workflows without blaming reviewers.
3. **How it works:** Explain how Claim Clarity brings relevant evidence and reasoning into the review process.
4. **Product experience:** Show a decision card, evidence trace, confidence context, and human review in realistic examples.
5. **Benefits:** Organize messages around connected intelligence, clear reasoning, human-centered decisions, and operational readiness.
6. **Trust:** Explain data handling, governance, limitations, and human oversight using approved, specific information.
7. **Audience pathways:** Help reviewers, operations leaders, and technology teams find relevant use cases.
8. **Call to action:** Offer a concrete next step, such as a product walkthrough or conversation with the team.

Keep the first screen concise. Support claims with product evidence and avoid medical, financial, or performance promises that have not been validated and approved.

## Design Governance

- Treat this guide as the shared source for positioning and design decisions; keep product patterns aligned with the same principles.
- Review new language, visual patterns, and product claims with the relevant product, design, engineering, and marketing owners.
- Include accessibility, responsive behavior, and dark/light theme checks in design review.
- Validate research and performance statements with their source, date, audience, and limitations before publication.
- Record exceptions and rationale when a product constraint requires a deviation; revisit them as the product evolves.

## Implementation Roadmap

### 1. Align

Confirm the category, target audiences, approved vocabulary, and any claims that require evidence or review.

### 2. Apply to product

Audit key workflows for decision-card consistency, evidence traceability, confidence context, accessible color use, and visible human review.

### 3. Apply to marketing

Update the website and sales materials to follow the messaging structure and support each capability or outcome with approved proof.

### 4. Validate and maintain

Test comprehension and accessibility with representative users. Review the guide when product capabilities, evidence, or market positioning materially change.
